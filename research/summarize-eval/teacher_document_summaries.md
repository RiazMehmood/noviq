## R1 — Removal from Service (Special Powers) Sindh Ordinance, 2000  *(doc_type: act)*

This Ordinance gives the Sindh Government special powers to remove, dismiss, or compulsorily retire
persons in government or corporate service for misconduct, corruption, inefficiency, or being found
otherwise unsuitable to continue. It sets out who the "competent authority" is and the grounds on
which action may be initiated against an employee.

It lays down the procedure for taking action: an inquiry officer or inquiry committee may be
appointed to investigate the allegations, with defined powers to summon evidence, fix hearings, and
report findings; the competent authority then passes final orders based on those findings. Pending
the proceedings, the employee may be placed under suspension with immediate effect, or instead
directed to proceed on admissible leave.

The Ordinance also protects the affected person's rights of representation and review, and gives a
right of appeal: a person aggrieved by a final order may appeal to the Sindh Service Tribunal within
a fixed period. It addresses continuation of pending proceedings regardless of the employee's
service status, pension and benefit consequences for those removed or retired, and the
Government's power to make rules. It expressly overrides other conflicting laws in force.

**Key areas the summary MUST hit:** special removal powers + grounds; competent authority; inquiry
procedure; suspension/leave during proceedings; representation/review; appeal to the Service
Tribunal; overriding effect; rule-making. *(Watch: don't invent specific penalty scales — this
Ordinance is about removal powers and process, not a graded penalty menu.)*

## R2 — Sindh Public Procurement (SPPRA) Rules, 2010 (revised 2013)  *(doc_type: rules)*

These Rules govern how public bodies in Sindh procure goods, works, and services, administered under
the Sindh Public Procurement Regulatory Authority. They establish the core principles of public
procurement — fairness, transparency, competition, economy, and value for money — and set the scope
and application of the rules across procuring agencies.

They regulate the whole procurement cycle: obtaining prior approval before procurement, choosing an
appropriate method of bidding (open, restricted, negotiated), and the response times bidders must be
given, distinguishing national from international competitive bidding. They cover pre-qualification
and eligibility of suppliers and contractors, the contents of bidding documents, bid submission and
opening, and evaluation and award criteria. Financial safeguards are provided through bid security
and performance security requirements tied to contract value.

The Rules also provide integrity and accountability mechanisms: mandatory record-keeping of
procurement proceedings, a process for handling complaints through a redressal committee and appeal
to a review committee, and blacklisting of suppliers, contractors, or consultants who breach
contracts or fail to perform. A dedicated part governs the selection and engagement of consultants,
including selection methods, evaluation of quality, and professional liability. The Rules assert
their precedence over other government procurement instructions.

**Key areas the summary MUST hit:** procurement principles + scope; methods of bidding + response
times; pre-qualification/eligibility; bid & performance security; evaluation/award; record-keeping;
complaints → review-committee appeal; blacklisting; consultants; overriding effect.

## R3 — Sindh Civil Servants (Efficiency & Discipline) Rules, 1973  *(doc_type: rules)*

These Rules govern disciplinary action against Sindh civil servants for inefficiency, misconduct,
corruption, or subversive activities. They define the grounds on which a civil servant may be
penalised and identify the authorities empowered to act.

They set out the penalties that may be imposed, distinguishing minor penalties from major penalties
(the major ones extending to reduction, compulsory retirement, removal, and dismissal). They then
prescribe the procedure that must be followed: the authorised officer frames a charge and a
statement of allegations, informs the accused, and gives an opportunity to reply; where an inquiry
is held, an Inquiry Officer or Inquiry Committee conducts it with the powers of a civil court to
summon witnesses and take evidence, and reports findings before a penalty is imposed.

The Rules cover situations where the normal inquiry does not apply (for example, conviction on a
criminal charge), suspension of a civil servant during proceedings, and the handling of officers on
deputation. They provide for representation and appeal, including to the Chief Minister, against
orders passed under the Rules.

**Key areas the summary MUST hit:** grounds for action; minor vs major penalties; charge +
statement of allegations; inquiry by Inquiry Officer/Committee with civil-court powers;
exceptions (e.g. conviction); suspension; right of representation/appeal.

## R4 — Sindh Civil Servants (Appointment, Promotion & Transfer / APT) Rules, 1974  *(doc_type: rules)*

These Rules govern the appointment, promotion, and transfer of civil servants in Sindh. Their core
scheme is to fix, for each range of Basic Pay Scales, the authority competent to make appointments —
for example lower scales are filled by the Head of Office or drawing-and-disbursing officer, middle
scales by regional or departmental heads, and higher scales (BS-16, BS-18 and above) by the
Secretary concerned or the Chief Minister.

They set eligibility conditions for appointment, including citizenship of Pakistan and domicile in
the Province, and address appointment on a current-charge or additional-charge basis. A parallel set
of provisions governs transfers, specifying which authorities may transfer officers in particular
scales and posts (such as Section Officers, Deputy Commissioners, and senior police officers) and
the limits on those transfer powers.

**Key areas the summary MUST hit:** appointment authority mapped to Basic Pay Scales; promotion;
transfer authorities by scale/post; eligibility (citizenship + domicile); current/additional charge.

## R5 — Sindh Delegation of Financial Powers, 2019  *(doc_type: rules)*

This instrument delegates financial and administrative powers within the Government of Sindh,
specifying which authority may exercise which powers and to what extent. Its central mechanism is a
delegation from the authorities listed in one column of its schedules to the subordinate authorities
in another column, with higher authorities able to exercise the powers of those below them.

It defines the financial powers of a wide range of functionaries — administrative secretaries,
special and additional secretaries, heads of attached departments, and numerous specific officers
across departments (health, police, works, irrigation, legal, and others) — describing the scope of
sanction and expenditure powers attached to each role. It includes a mechanism for the Government to
resolve ambiguities or remove difficulties in applying the powers by issuing necessary orders.

**Key areas the summary MUST hit:** delegation of financial/sanction powers; column-to-column
delegation scheme; higher authority exercising subordinate powers; powers of secretaries and
listed departmental officers; removal-of-difficulties power. *(This doc is schedule/table-heavy;
a faithful summary describes the delegation SCHEME, not a list of every officer.)*

## R6 — Sindh Rules of Business, 1986  *(doc_type: rules)*

These Rules govern how the business of the Government of Sindh is organised and transacted. They
allocate government business among the various departments and define the roles and responsibilities
of ministers, secretaries, and other officers in disposing of that business.

They regulate secretariat procedure: how cases are processed and submitted, which matters require
reference to the Chief Minister, the Cabinet, the Governor, or other departments, and the
coordination required between departments (for example with the Finance and Law departments) before
decisions are taken. They cover the conduct of official business, custody of records, and the
procedures that ensure decisions are taken by the proper authority.

**Key areas the summary MUST hit:** allocation of business among departments; roles of
ministers/secretaries; secretariat case-handling procedure; inter-departmental consultation
(Finance/Law); references to CM/Cabinet/Governor. *(Watch: keep it to the business-allocation and
procedure genre; don't invent specific rule contents I haven't verified.)*

## R7 — Constitution of the Islamic Republic of Pakistan  *(doc_type: act; 12 Parts, 283 articles indexed)*

The Constitution is the supreme law of the Islamic Republic of Pakistan. It establishes the country
as a federal parliamentary democracy, declares the supremacy of the Constitution and the sovereignty
of Almighty Allah, and sets out the foundational objectives and framework within which the State and
all its organs must function.

It guarantees Fundamental Rights to citizens — such as security of person, safeguards on arrest and
detention, freedom of movement, assembly, association, speech, religion, and the rights to equality
before the law and non-discrimination — and makes laws inconsistent with those rights void. Alongside
these enforceable rights it lays down Principles of Policy that guide the State. It then defines the
structure of the Federation: the Parliament (the National Assembly and the Senate), their
composition, powers, and law-making procedure; the Federal Executive headed by the President and the
Prime Minister and Cabinet; and the distribution of legislative and financial powers between the
Federation and the Provinces.

The Constitution establishes the Judicature — the Supreme Court, High Courts, and the Federal Shariat
Court — defining their jurisdiction and the independence of the judiciary, and it provides for the
provincial governments mirroring the federal structure. Further parts govern Islamic provisions and
the requirement that laws conform to the injunctions of Islam, financial and revenue matters
including the procedure for money bills and the audit of accounts, the conduct of elections and the
Election Commission, emergency provisions, services of Pakistan, and the procedure for amending the
Constitution itself. Throughout, many articles carry a history of substitution and insertion by
successive constitutional amendments.

**Key areas the summary MUST hit:** supremacy of the Constitution + federal parliamentary character;
Fundamental Rights (+ Principles of Policy); Parliament (National Assembly + Senate) & legislative
procedure; Federal Executive (President/PM/Cabinet); federal–provincial distribution of powers;
Judicature (Supreme Court/High Courts/Federal Shariat Court); provinces; Islamic provisions;
finance/elections/emergency/amendment procedure. *(This is broad by nature; a 2–3 segment summary is
expected. Watch: the pre-baked builder reduces over ~283 article gists in ~3 segments — check the
segments read in Part order and none are dropped.)*
