Free GPU Cloud Credits

Programs, eligibility, and how long they actually last for one department of ~100 users

Noviq internal · Data current July 2026 · Companion to LLM Provider & Model Analysis (July 2026)

The single most important finding, before any table.

The question "how long will free credits last for 100 users?" contains a hidden assumption that does not hold: GPU credits are not consumed by users or by tokens — they are consumed by wall-clock time the instance exists. A rented GPU bills identically whether it serves 100 queries or zero.

Measured against Noviq's actual load (§3), 100 users keep a single mid-range GPU busy roughly 5% of the day. If that instance runs 24/7 you are paying for about 20 hours of idle for every hour of work. Consequently the operating pattern changes your runway by 3–10× — far more than the choice of provider, GPU, or credit programme does. §5 quantifies this; §6 is the resulting recommendation.

3. Sizing the actual load — 1 department, ~100 users

Stated assumptions, so the arithmetic can be corrected if any is wrong:

AssumptionValueBasis
Licensed users in the department100Given.
Daily active share30%Typical internal-tool engagement; 30 users/day.
Queries per active user per day10Research-style usage.
Queries per day300Derived.
Prompt tokens per query~8,000Measured Noviq RAG prompt (retrieved passages + grounding prefix).
Generated tokens per answer~500Current answer lengths.
GPU seconds per query~15 s~3 s prefill + ~12 s decode on a 24 GB card.
GPU-hours of real compute per day~1.25 h300 × 15 s.
Peak simultaneous requests3–5300 queries clustered into office hours.

What this means. One department of 100 users needs about 1.25 GPU-hours of compute per day — roughly 5% utilisation of a single 24 GB card running around the clock. A single L4 or A10G is not merely sufficient; it is already over-provisioned. The constraint is peak concurrency (3–5 simultaneous requests, which vLLM batches comfortably), never total throughput.

The practical implication: scaling from 100 to 300 users would not require a second GPU. The cost model is dominated by uptime, not headcount — so do not price additional departments as additional GPUs until concurrency, not volume, actually forces it.

4. Runway — how long each credit pot lasts

Runway on a GCP L4 at $0.70/hr, the recommended target. Three operating patterns:

  • 24/7 — instance always up (730 h/month, $511/month).
  • Business hours — up 10 h/day × 22 days (220 h/month, $154/month). Requires a start/stop schedule.
  • Autoscale-to-zero — instance runs only when serving (~40 h/month, ~$28/month). Requires tolerating cold starts.

5. Why the operating pattern dominates

Same $300, same GPU, same 100 users — runway varies 17× purely by uptime policy:

A scheduled stop/start is a cron job and a systemd unit — a few hours of work that multiplies every credit pot in §4 by roughly 3.3×. It is the highest-return item in this document.

6. Recommendations

  1. Apply to NVIDIA Inception this week. No fee, no equity, no funding minimum, no deadline — and it gates the AWS and Nebius tiers. It is the highest-value action available and costs nothing but the application.
  2. Take the $300 Google Cloud trial now for the pilot. It needs no application, and an L4 at $0.70/hr is the best price/fit match for Qwen3-8B.
  3. Implement scheduled start/stop before spending any credits. It multiplies every pot by ~3.3× and is a few hours of work. Do this first, not after the credits start draining.
  4. Do not provision an A100 or H100. At 5% utilisation of an L4, larger cards buy nothing but burn rate. Revisit only if the model grows beyond ~13B or concurrency exceeds ~20.
  5. Size the second department by concurrency, not headcount. One L4 should absorb several departments. Do not price additional GPUs into the commercial model until measured peak concurrency demands it.
  6. Confirm the data-boundary position before committing to a foreign hyperscaler. A government department's corpus moving to AWS/GCP is a policy question, not a cost question. The NTC ↔ Data Vault government GPUaaS arrangement already noted in our business planning would resolve both the boundary story and the GPU cost — get pricing from them before locking in a cloud, because that route may make this entire analysis moot.

Caveats. Credit amounts are what programmes advertise; actual awards vary with reviewer discretion and are frequently lower than the headline. Most credits exclude some services and carry 1–2 year expiries. GPU quota is a separate approval from credits on AWS, GCP and Azure, and often takes days — apply for quota early. All hourly rates are on-demand US-region list prices from July 2026 and vary by region.
