# Beta Launch — Audience Research: Roles, Pain Points, Positioning

Research basis for the beta-launch deck. Compiled 2026-07-26 from web research plus
the actual indexed corpus on the production VM. **Read the two corrections in §1
before writing any slide** — both invalidate assumptions we started with.

---

## 1. Two corrections to our starting assumptions

### 1.1 This is Sindh, not the Federal Government

Confirmed from the live corpus on the VM (`/opt/noviq/**/*.pdf`), not inferred:

| # | Instrument |
|---|---|
| 01 | Sindh Government Rules of Business 1986 |
| 02 | SPPRA Rules 2010 (Revised 2013) |
| 03 | Sindh Public Procurement Act 2009 |
| 04 | Sindh Local Government Act 2013 |
| 05 | Compendium of Rules under SLGA 2013 |
| 06 | Manual for Development Projects 2019 (PC-I) |
| 09 | Sindh Delegation of Financial Powers & Financial Control Rules 2019 |
| 11 | Sindh Civil Servants (APT) Rules 1974 |
| — | Sindh Civil Servants (Efficiency & Discipline) Rules 1973 |
| — | Removal from Service (Special Powers) Sindh Ordinance 2000 |
| — | Govt. of Sindh Manual of Secretariat Instructions 2010 |
| — | Constitution of Pakistan |

**Consequences for the deck.** Use Sindh instruments and Sindh designations
throughout. "Minister" means *Provincial* Minister. The apex personas are Chief
Secretary and Additional Chief Secretary, not a federal Secretary. Nothing federal
is in scope — **PPRA Rules 2004 and the federal E&D rules are not loaded**, so the
room must not be allowed to assume they are.

⚠️ **Sequence gaps at 07, 08 and 10.** Documents were intended there and are absent.
Confirm what they were before making any coverage claim.

### 1.2 e-Office already exists — and it is the objection that will kill the pitch

Pakistan's e-Office is deployed across **all federal ministries and divisions**
(42 organisations, 6,760 users, 67,998 electronic files, 407,097 documents), with
claimed reductions of **up to 80% in processing time** and Rs 300 million saved in
stationery. Sindh runs comparable initiatives.

If Noviq is presented as "digitises files" or "speeds up file movement", it will be
heard as *"we already bought that"* and the meeting is over.

**The distinction to make explicitly, early, on its own slide:**

> e-Office moves the file. It does not tell you **which rule applies** to what is
> inside the file. Noviq answers the question the file is about.

Pre-empt this. Do not wait to be ambushed with it.

## 2. The strongest pain point found — and it is legal exposure, not efficiency

This reframes the entire value proposition, so it deserves to be the spine of the
deck rather than one scenario among four.

### 2.1 Proceedings under the wrong instrument are set aside in full

Sindh departments have repeatedly run complete disciplinary processes — charge
sheet, departmental inquiry, penalty order, final order — under the **Sindh Civil
Servants (E&D) Rules 1973** when the **Removal from Service (Special Powers) Sindh
Ordinance 2000** applied, or the reverse.

The Sindh High Court's position: where action was initiated and carried to
conclusion *"under a misconception of law and under a wrong law"*, it **vitiated the
entire proceedings**, including the final order, which could not be sustained.

**Both instruments are in Noviq's corpus.** This is precisely a "which of these two
governs my case" question — the shape of question Noviq is built to answer.

### 2.2 Vague show-cause notices vitiate on natural-justice grounds

A show-cause notice must state the charges and the specific grounds. The documented
common failure is a notice too vague for the accused to mount a defence; the Service
Tribunal treats a defective notice as violating natural justice and vitiating the
process. Penalties are *frequently* set aside where the rules were not fully
complied with.

Noviq's **Notice Assist is user-verified working** on exactly this class of task.

### 2.3 Why this matters for the pitch

For a Secretary or Chief Secretary, **an order that survives challenge is worth far
more than minutes saved.** Reinstatement with back pay after a set-aside is a
budget hit, a reputational hit, and a file that has to be run twice. Lead with
avoided exposure; treat time saved as a secondary benefit.

## 3. Audit objections — quantified, repeating, and preventable

The Director/Secretary pain, and the one with hard public numbers attached.

**Federal scale (AGP 2025–26 audit of civil accounts, 399 pages):**
- **Rs 3,177 billion** in unapproved supplementary grants, ~92% bypassing
  parliamentary approval.
- Ministry of Interior & Narcotics Control: **65 audit paras** (highest), followed
  by HEC (31), TDAP (18), National Food Security (17), Science & Technology (16).
- Health: **Rs 3.41 billion** in irregularities, of which **Rs 1,779.26 million**
  procurement-related.
- Rs 1.2 billion spent overhauling two helicopters **without open competitive
  bidding**.

**Sindh, and the detail that matters most:**
- Audit of Sindh Union Committees/Councils: *"Violation of Rules"* in **14 cases,
  Rs 194.884 million**; pre-audit *"almost non-functional"*; and critically —
  **"similar nature of internal control violations reported year after year."**
- **SPPRA Rule 17(1):** procurements over Rs 100,000 and up to Rs 1 million must be
  advertised by timely notification on the Authority's website, and may be in print
  media. Departments repeatedly awarded work **without inviting open tenders or
  advertising at all**.

**The argument this hands us.** "Reported year after year" means the rule is not in
dispute — the *knowledge of it* is simply not reaching the officer at the moment of
decision. That is a retrieval problem, which is exactly what Noviq is. A specific,
numeric, frequently-breached threshold rule also makes an ideal demo: precise
question, precise cited answer.

## 4. Institutional memory — the Chief Secretary's argument

- Policy provides a **normal minimum tenure of 3 years** with 3 months' notice.
- Practice: secretaries and district-level postings **often last under one year**;
  officials are "lucky" to exceed 12 months.
- Frequent transfers, loyalty-based postings and a culture of obedience over
  professionalism are documented as having weakened institutions.

**Every rotation resets the department's working knowledge of its own rules.** The
officer leaves; the rules stay; the understanding of how they interlock goes with the
officer.

Noviq is the only asset in that cycle that does not get transferred. This is the
argument for an **institutional** purchase rather than a personal productivity tool —
and it speaks directly to SGA&CD, which owns postings and transfers.

## 5. Political cover — a recommendation already approved but never implemented

The **National Commission on Government Reforms (NCGR, Dr Ishrat Husain, 2007)**
recommended training staff in *"electronic filing, messaging, sharing and exchanging
of documents, retrieving, reporting and archiving."* The report was finalised during
a political transition, ownership stayed *"uncertain"*, and what the later task force
produced *"remained restricted to government files only."*

**Use this.** Positioning Noviq as delivering a long-approved but unimplemented
reform lets a Minister support it as **continuity rather than novelty** — materially
lower political risk than backing something new.

## 6. Revised persona set

Research changed this. The original four missed the gatekeeper, and conflated
federal with provincial titles.

| Persona | What the role actually does | Core pain | What they need to hear |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Section Officer** | Head of a section; controls assistants, clerks, steno-typists, record managers. Does the examination, **noting and drafting**. | Which of 12 instruments governs this case; is the provision current; will my note survive the Secretary's scrutiny and the auditor's. | "You will not be the person who cited the wrong rule." |
| **Deputy Secretary** | Chief of a **group of sections**. Upward and downward communication routes through this office. | Everything reaching the Secretary carries his endorsement; he must catch subordinates' errors without re-doing the research himself. | "Check a note against the rule in seconds, not an afternoon." |
| **Secretary** | Administrative head of the department; accountable for it. | Audit paras, litigation, set-aside orders, inconsistent interpretation between his own sections. | "Fewer repeat audit paras. Orders that hold up." |
| **Additional Chief Secretary / Chief Secretary** | Apex of the provincial bureaucracy; SGA&CD owns postings/promotions/transfers. | Cross-department inconsistency; knowledge lost at every rotation. | "Institutional memory that does not get transferred." |
| **Provincial Minister** | Political head; accountable publicly. | Decision latency; defensibility; public embarrassment. | "Faster decisions you can defend, on a reform already approved in 2007." |

**⚠️ User ≠ buyer.** The purchase decision sits with Chief Secretary / Secretary.
Adoption depends entirely on Section Officers finding it genuinely useful. The deck
must serve both, and the demo must be credible to the officer in the room who will
actually have to use it — that person will spot a staged answer instantly.

## 7. Scope honesty — what the corpus supports

**Supported today:**

| Domain | Instruments |
|---|---|
| Discipline / misconduct | E&D Rules 1973 + RSO 2000 |
| Appointment, promotion, transfer | Sindh Civil Servants (APT) Rules 1974 |
| Procurement | SPPA 2009 + SPPRA Rules 2010 (rev. 2013) |
| Financial sanction | Delegation of Financial Powers 2019 |
| Development projects | Manual for Development Projects 2019 (PC-I) |
| Local government | SLGA 2013 + Compendium |
| Secretariat procedure | Manual of Secretariat Instructions 2010; RoB 1986 |
| Constitutional | Constitution of Pakistan |

**Not supported — state this plainly:** anything federal (PPRA Rules 2004, federal
E&D), taxation, police rules, subject-specific departmental manuals, and the missing
documents at sequence positions 07/08/10.

## 8. Scenario recommendation

Four were selected. Research suggests one substitution and one reordering.

1. **Disciplinary proceeding — E&D 1973 vs RSO 2000.** Promote to anchor scenario.
   Highest stakes, documented court consequence, Notice Assist already verified.
2. **Delegation of Financial Powers — "can I sanction this at my level?"** *New
   recommendation.* Highest daily frequency of any question here, numeric and
   auditable, squarely in the corpus, and low demo risk.
3. **Procurement threshold — SPPRA Rule 17(1).** Documented repeat violation with a
   precise numeric answer. Excellent demo.
4. **Posting / transfer / seniority — APT Rules 1974.** High volume; demonstrates the
   fragmented-instrument problem we already fixed.
5. **Summary / noting drafting.** Keep, but **last** and framed as direction-of-travel
   — drafting is less proven than retrieval, and over-promising it is the easiest way
   to lose credibility in month two.

---

## 9. The deck's spine

One paragraph that reaches all five personas simultaneously:

> Your rules are not in doubt. Your officers are capable. But knowing *which* rule
> applies — in what version, at what threshold, under which of two overlapping
> instruments — is scattered across twelve documents, and it walks out of the
> building every eleven months. That gap is what produces set-aside orders, the same
> audit paras year after year, and litigation you should have won. Noviq closes that
> gap, and attaches the citation.
